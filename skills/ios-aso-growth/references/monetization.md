# Monetization: making the call

Decide from the product's job, how often it's used, the audience size, and what the
privacy promise costs. Write the decision and its reasons in the strategy page and the
roadmap's rejected list, so it isn't re-litigated every release.

## The options

| Model | Fits | Costs | Typical numbers (2026, check live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free with ads | Casual, high-frequency, large audience (games, puzzles, content) | Tracking/ATT prompt, privacy label "Data Used to Track You", worse UX, editors less likely to feature | Ad revenue per daily user is cents per day, so it needs very large volume |
| Ads + "remove ads" IAP ($0.99–$4.99) | The same apps, as a cheap upgrade path | Anchors the price low; teaches users that "paid" means "fewer annoyances" | Conversion is usually low single-digit percent |
| Paid up front | Niche pro tools with strong word of mouth | No trial means low conversion from search; hard to rank | — |
| Freemium + subscription | Tools used weekly or daily with ongoing value | Must keep earning it; paywalled basics get punished in reviews | Loggers $24–40/yr; coach apps $60–180/yr |
| Freemium + subscription + lifetime | The same, for privacy-minded or subscription-averse buyers | Lifetime is a liability only if you pay for servers | Lifetime ≈ 2.5–3× annual |

## Decision rules

1. **If the brand promises privacy or "no account", don't ship ads.** The "Data Not
   Collected" label is proof a competitor can't copy without rebuilding; an ad SDK destroys it.
2. **If the core job happens under time pressure** (between sets, while cooking, while
   driving), ads interrupt the moment the app exists for. Don't ship ads.
3. **If users come back daily for a tool, sell the tool,** not the absence of ads.
4. **Free tier:** at least as generous as the category leader's on the core job (no caps on
   logging, routines or history). Paywalled basics are the most-cited complaint in fitness
   app reviews.
5. **Pro sells what only you do**, not quantities of the basics.
6. **Prices:** sit inside the cluster for your category, near the leaders. Don't undercut the
   leader by a dollar; win on what they can't do. First test after about 4 weeks of data: a
   longer trial on the annual plan.
7. **Lifetime** when there are no per-user server costs.
8. **Never** add a tip jar, "remove ads" or a cheaper tier as an afterthought; each one
   reframes what Pro is worth.

## Reps (2026-11-21): the call and why

Free notebook (all logging, programs, insights, Watch, imports and exports) + Pro trainer
($3.99/mo, $29.99/yr with a 7-day trial, $79.99 lifetime). No ads and no $0.99 remove-ads:
the app is privacy-first (rule 1), used mid-set (rule 2) and daily (rule 3), and a $0.99
unlock would sit 30× under the annual plan. Coaches pay for multi-athlete coaching;
athletes never pay to be coached.
