# Caption formulas and the evidence behind them

Researched 2026-11-21. "Verified" = read at the source; "Hypothesis" = repeated widely,
no primary source found.

## What the evidence supports

- Apple: the first 1–3 screenshots appear in search results when there's no app
  preview, "so make sure these highlight the essence of your app"; each later screenshot
  should focus "on a main benefit or feature"; include a Dark Mode screenshot if
  supported (developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page, verified).
- Only about 13–33% of visitors scroll the screenshot gallery (StoreMaven decks; Sensor
  Tower 2015 case study 17%; AppAgent 13%). Frames 1–2 carry the decision.
- Shorter, experience-led captions beat feature lists in SplitMetrics case studies
  (ZiMAD +32%, FreshPlanet about +10%); no clean, isolated benefit-vs-feature test is
  public. Direction consistent, size uncertain.
- The top 200 apps average about 6 words and 35 characters per caption on frames 1–3
  (Launchmatic, informal survey).
- Whether Apple indexes caption text for search is **not proven** (Appfigures claimed
  it in June 2025; ConsultMyApp/APPlyzer and AppTweak found no strong evidence). Use
  search words in captions anyway: they help people recognise the job.
- Review Guidelines 2.3.3 (show the app in use), 2.3.7/2.3.14 (no prices, no other
  apps' names, no unverifiable claims), 2.3.10 (no other platforms) apply to screenshots.

## Formulas that fit 3–6 words

| Formula | Template | Works | Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job to be done | [Verb] [the job] | Know what to lift next | Smart Workout Engine |
| Speed with a number | [Verb] [thing] in [qty] | Log every set in one tap | Fast logging |
| Loss aversion | Never [lose/miss] [thing they value] | Never lose a set or a PR | Reliable sync |
| Their moment, as a question | [Situation]? [Verb] [outcome]. | Slept 5 hours? Lift lighter. | Recovery-aware plans |
| Before → after | No more [old pain] | No more gym notebook | Digital workout journal |
| Ownership | [Verb] your [thing] | Bring your history with you | Import (CSV) |
| Proof (once you have it) | [Number] [peer group] [verb] | 4.9★ from 10,000 lifters | Loved by users |

## Leaders' opening lines (US App Store descriptions, 2026-11-21, via the iTunes Lookup API)

- Hevy: "Log every set, follow any program or split, and track your lifts and volume." (after proof: "trusted by 15M+ lifters", "Rated 4.9")
- StrengthLog: "Plan your training. Log every set. Track your progress."
- Setgraph: "the fast, no-fuss way to track your lifts and actually achieve progressive overload. Log a set in seconds…"
- Gravl: "…tells you exactly how much weight to lift next time."
- Strong: "The most intuitive workout and exercise tracker…" (adjective-led: weakest)

The strongest pattern: proof first when you have it, then bare imperatives in the user's
own category words (log, track, progress, "progressive overload", "what weight to lift next").

## Failure modes

Internal feature names; describing UI ("Swipe to see charts"); vague adjectives
("powerful", "intuitive", "seamless"); puns that don't read at thumbnail size; claims
nobody cares about ("built with SwiftUI", "AI-powered" with no payoff); unsourced "#1";
prices in screenshots; the best message after frame 3; a splash screen as frame 1.
